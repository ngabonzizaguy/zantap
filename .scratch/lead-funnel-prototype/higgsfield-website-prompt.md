# Copy/paste prompt for Higgsfield

Design and build a premium, award-level lead-generation website for **ZanTap**.
ZanTap is a restaurant commerce and operations service launching first in
Rwanda, then expanding across East Africa.

## The business in one sentence

ZanTap gives a restaurant its own mobile ordering link, helps customers pay by
Mobile Money, sends paid orders to a live kitchen dashboard, and helps the team
move each order from new to ready and delivered.

## The audience

The primary visitor is a restaurant owner or manager in Rwanda. They may run a
restaurant, café, grill, bakery, bar, cloud kitchen, takeaway, or small chain.
They are busy and may not use technical software language. Many will arrive on
a phone from Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, X, or WhatsApp.

The page must be understandable within five seconds. Use short sentences,
familiar words, obvious actions, and visual proof. Keep Rwanda as the launch
market, but do not make the brand feel limited to one country. The system should
feel ready for Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania, and the rest of East Africa.

## The offer

**Free guided setup for selected early restaurant partners.**

The visitor receives:

- Their own restaurant ordering link.
- Help adding their first menu items.
- Mobile Money payment setup.
- A live kitchen order dashboard.
- A personal walkthrough on WhatsApp.
- No long contract and no pressure to continue.

Primary CTA: **Get my free setup**

Secondary CTA: **See how ZanTap works**

## Core message

Suggested headline: **More orders. One clear flow.**

Suggested supporting copy: **Customers order and pay from their phone. Your
kitchen sees every paid order in one place.**

Do not use long SaaS paragraphs. Avoid words such as omnichannel, ecosystem,
digitisation, orchestration, infrastructure, and seamless. Show concrete
outcomes instead.

## Required story

Tell the story in this order:

1. A customer opens the restaurant’s link.
2. They choose food and pay with Mobile Money.
3. The paid order appears in the kitchen.
4. The team moves it through preparing, ready, and delivery.
5. The owner can see today’s orders and sales.

Each step should use one short headline and one short sentence.

## Product imagery — non-negotiable

Use the supplied real ZanTap UI screenshots as product truth. Do not paste them
inside ordinary cards, browser windows, floating rectangles, or tilted boxes.

Present them inside professionally built devices:

- Realistic premium phone hardware with a believable bezel, speaker or dynamic
  island, side buttons, glass reflection, controlled shadow, and correct screen
  clipping.
- Use a tablet or kitchen display device for the operational dashboard when it
  improves the composition.
- Preserve the screenshot aspect ratio and legibility. Do not distort, rewrite,
  regenerate, or invent interface content.
- Use one strong hero device composition rather than a pile of competing
  screenshots.
- Device perspective must be intentional. It must never cover a person’s face
  or obscure the primary CTA.
- On mobile, devices must remain large enough to understand and must not become
  tiny decorative thumbnails.

Use these real screen roles:

- Customer storefront: shows what restaurant customers receive.
- MoMo setup: proves payments are locally relevant.
- Menu creation: proves setup is simple.
- Store-live QR screen: proves a restaurant can launch and share its link.
- Kitchen orders screen: proves paid orders arrive in a clear operational view.

Never use screens that expose a real customer or driver phone number.

## Visual direction

Create a premium editorial product experience, not a generic SaaS template.

- Off-white, warm black, cream, natural amber, and restrained atmospheric color.
- Sophisticated light serif display type paired with a highly legible sans-serif.
- Large typography, generous negative space, disciplined grids, and strong
  alignment.
- Mobile-first, but visually exceptional on desktop.
- Motion should feel cinematic and controlled: slow device parallax, soft light
  movement, subtle screen transitions, and deliberate scroll reveals.
- Use 3D depth where it supports the product: device materials, a sculpted
  pedestal, tap-signal rings, or a restrained order-flow object.
- A subtle Rwanda restaurant atmosphere can appear in one hero direction, but
  avoid flags, tourist imagery, clichés, generic “African patterns,” neon
  fintech visuals, or staged handshake photography.
- Respect reduced-motion settings and provide still-image fallbacks.

## Lead capture

The main form should feel short and safe. Capture:

- Name
- Restaurant name
- WhatsApp number
- City
- The main thing they want to improve

Show a clear success state and an optional **Continue on WhatsApp** action.
Automatically retain campaign and UTM attribution.

## Private lead dashboard

Create a separate, secure operator view where ZanTap can see:

- Name, restaurant, WhatsApp number, email if supplied, city, source, campaign,
  status, and submission time.
- Totals for new, contacted, qualified, and converted leads.
- Search and filters.
- Lead notes and follow-up date.
- WhatsApp, call, and email actions.
- CSV/Excel export.

The dashboard must not visually compete with the public funnel.

## Required output

First propose **three structurally different art directions**. They must differ
in layout and storytelling, not only color or wording:

1. Cinematic restaurant story with one hero device.
2. Clean editorial product stage with a sculptural device composition.
3. Conversion-first founder offer with the lead form visible early.

Then build the strongest direction as a complete responsive website with real
interactions and polished mobile behavior.

## Do not do this

- Do not create a generic hero followed by three feature cards.
- Do not scatter screenshots in random boxes.
- Do not generate fake ZanTap interface screens.
- Do not hide weak composition behind gradients or excessive animation.
- Do not use fake testimonials, fake customer logos, or invented growth data.
- Do not make every section look the same.
- Do not let decoration overpower the offer or lead form.

The final result should make a restaurant owner think: **“I understand this. I
can see it working in my restaurant. I know what to do next.”**
